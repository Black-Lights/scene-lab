# The avoid list: marks of machine-made design

F-004 points here. These are the marks that make a frame look generated by default rather than designed for its
topic. They come from published lists of such marks and from corrections on real videos made with this engine.

**Any item may stay when the topic gives a reason**: grid lines on a map, numbers on a real sequence, capitals in an
acronym, mono type for real code. Write that reason in `direction.why`. If the only reason is that it looks nice or
modern, take it out.

How to use it: design the look from the subject first (its materials, instruments, documents, places; SPEC.md
part 4), then read this list against a strip of your scenes. Then ask whether a similar video would get the same
design; where the answer is yes, change that part.

## Method

- **Look up how the subject's own field shows this kind of information** before drawing it: a chemist's diagram, a
  surveyor's plan, a circuit schematic, a museum label. Borrow its conventions, not a generic dashboard.
- **Removing the marks does not make a design.** A frame with none of the marks below can still be plain. Every
  video needs a point of view taken from its subject, and one thing the viewer remembers.
- **Spend boldness in one place.** One signature element per video, everything around it quiet.
- **Decide the shared parts first** (the type scale, the label style, the colours with a meaning), then the scenes,
  so that no two scenes solve the same thing two ways.

## Layout and structure

- A coloured stripe on one edge of a card or a row, or as the mark of a picked item. Mark a picked item with a
  wash, an outline or a bold name.
- Cards around everything, cards inside cards, grids of identical cards. Use rows, spacing, tone or dividers.
- The same rounded rectangle (8 to 20 px, one soft grey shadow) on everything.
- An icon tile above a heading, oversized icons, emoji as icons (F-004).
- Equal columns of heading, text and list: a row of feature cards without the icons.
- The same sequence of hero, numbers, feature grid and ending, whatever the content.
- A big number in a tile as decoration. Show a number on a scale, in a sentence or as label and value.
- Everything centred.
- Numbered 01 / 02 / 03 markers or step boxes on things that are not a real sequence.
- Position that means nothing, such as items placed at the corners only to fill them.
- Decorative grid backgrounds, repeating stripes, equal spacing everywhere.
- Hairlines, square corners and dense columns as a reflex: this broadsheet look is a default too.

## Type

- Inter, Geist, Space Grotesk or Instrument Serif by reflex. Choose a face with a reason from the subject.
- A serif italic or one coloured word in a headline, gradient text.
- Uppercase, letter-spaced labels on every block. Use sentence case; capitals are for real acronyms.
- A small badge or label above every heading. The title comes first, anything else under it.
- Monospace for a technical look. Mono is for data values and code only, never for a sentence or a label.
- Flat hierarchy, crushed tracking, tight line height, justified text, long lines.
- Title Case on headings and labels; a headline that runs past about five lines.
- Text too small to read on a phone at the video's size.

## Colour and surface

- Purple-to-blue gradients, lavender, cyan on near-black, neon accents on dark.
- Warm cream with a high-contrast serif and terracotta.
- Near-black with one acid accent, or dark with grey text.
- Large glows, radial halos, blurred shapes, coloured shadows, frosted glass. A "futuristic" topic does not license
  them.
- Gradients as decoration.
- A pure #000 or #FFF ground.
- Grey text on a coloured fill, text that barely passes contrast (make.py warns).
- Colour as decoration, or colour as the only signal. Say a state with shape, word and colour together.

## Words on screen and in the narration

- Em dashes and en dashes, "not X but Y" lines, clever openers and closers, lists of three by reflex.
- Sales lines. Say what it is, how it works and what was measured.
- Filler: "dive into", "landscape", "game-changer", "in today's world", "it's worth noting", "let's explore",
  vague superlatives.
- Strings joined by middle dots, labels built as a word, a long dash and a fragment.
- The same text twice in one frame, a subtitle that repeats the title, field names from data used as labels.
- Invented numbers, quotes or logos, and suspiciously round figures. Claim only what the source says (F-002).
- Narration at one pitch with no emphasis: write sentences a person would stress (F-013).

## Motion

- Motion that shows no real change: every move answers what changed (docs/design.md, motion language).
- Objects that slide without weight, bounce, elastic or spring overshoot, anything that loops forever.
- Layout that shifts while the viewer reads it.
- Moving backgrounds: they cost bitrate in every frame and pull the eye from the subject.

## Pictures

- Rough or placeholder illustrations, decorative sparklines, broken images.
- Several illustration styles in one video.
- First-hit stock photos: look at the contact sheet and pick a picture that fits the meaning (SPEC.md part 5).
- Library defaults left unchanged.
